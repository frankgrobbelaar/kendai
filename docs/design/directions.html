Three design languages for kendai

All three are Material 3 Expressive underneath, so Compose supplies the components, accessibility and motion. They differ in colour, type, shape and motion. Washi is chosen: its board shows the library in light and dark, a book, a game guide, e-ink mode and a tablet.

Quiet and bookishChosen

The handmade paper Japanese books are printed on. Warm paper and ink, a vermilion seal for the accent, serif headings, hairline rules and calm, page-like motion. The closest to a printed book.

墨Sumiink

Focused and minimal

Ink on stone. Dark first, for reading at night, in quiet greys with one jade accent. Thin outlines instead of shadows, page numbers in mono, and chrome that steps right out of the way. The nearest to e-ink in spirit.

What all three share

Material 3 Expressive underneathCompose's components, accessibility and motion. A language changes only colour, type, shape and motion.
Light, dark and four reading themesThe app follows the device, with an override. Books get a paper, a sepia, a dark and a black theme in every language.
E-ink modeBlack and white with no motion, on by itself on e-ink readers. Each language keeps its type and shapes there.
Phones, tablets and split screenA rail and side panels on tablets, two pages side by side in landscape, and layouts that hold up in small windows.
Where you left off, everywhereProgress on every cover, Continue reading first in the library, and every book opening at its place.
Readable firstText at 4.5:1 or better, 48 dp touch targets, TalkBack labels, and dynamic colour as an option.
